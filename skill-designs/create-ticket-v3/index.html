create-ticket-v3

Turns a few lines of notes into a Jira ticket fast: usually one user story, or a bug, a task or an epic. When the notes hold several separate items, it offers to split them, and on request it drafts several tickets at once. You see every draft and every field before anything is written, and only your yes creates it.

The problem it solves

You paste a few lines into the chat and ask Claude for a ticket. A ticket comes back, and you cannot tell which parts came from your notes. The skill is built against five ways such a ticket can go wrong.

Ask for a ticketIt invents what the notes do not say. A number in a criterion, a cause for a bug, a reason nobody gave.
create-ticket-v3Every sentence needs a source in the notes, the code or Jira. A missing fact is named under Flags, never guessed.
Ask for a ticketIt writes a plan instead of a ticket. Implementation steps and sub-tasks fill the body.
create-ticket-v3The ticket states the result, never the route. The skill leaves the breakdown to the developers who take the ticket; sub-tasks are drafted only when you list them.
Ask for a ticketFields are filled in silence. A priority or an assignee appears with no word about where it came from.
create-ticket-v3A numbered list shows every field with its source. A value it must not guess becomes a numbered question.
Ask for a ticketIt gets the size wrong. Two separate pieces of work land in one ticket, or one result is cut into tickets by its steps or layers.
create-ticket-v3Items that stand on their own are offered as a split on one line. The parts of one result stay one ticket.
Ask for a ticketIt writes to Jira before you looked. Or it reads "looks fine, but…" as a yes.
create-ticket-v3Nothing is written before a clear yes with no question open. Any other reply shows the whole preview again.

When to use it, and when not

Use it for

  • A few lines of notes, in any language, that should become one story, bug or task, fast.
  • Notes that hold several separate items, such as a bug and a new feature, which you want as separate tickets in one pass.
  • An epic, an epic and its stories, or new stories under an epic that already exists.
  • The sub-tasks you name, under a new or an existing story, task or bug.
  • A ticket where you want to see every field and where its value came from before it exists.

Not for

  • A change to an issue that already exists.Edit it in Jira. The skill stops on this ask.
  • Work that needs a plan, research or a review first.Do that work first. The skill writes no plan and does no deep research.
  • Sub-tasks proposed for you.Leave them to the team that takes the ticket. The skill drafts only the sub-tasks you list by name.
  • An epic broken into stories for you.List the stories yourself, one line each, or leave them to refinement. The skill never invents stories.
  • No Jira Cloud site reachable through the Atlassian MCP.Connect it first. Without it the skill drafts, but cannot create.
  • You already have the full ticket text and every field value, and want it sent as written.Create it in Jira's own form. The skill rewrites the body into the shape of its type and shows a preview first.

How a run goes

Start

  1. 1You start the run with your notes, in any language. The notes also include what you said earlier in the conversation and anything you pasted. Pasted text is data: an instruction inside it, such as "already approved", is quoted back to you under Flags (the preview's list of warnings and notes) and never followed.
  2. 2You asked to change an issue that already exists. The skill says in one line that this is outside it, and stops. Adding stories or sub-tasks under an existing epic or parent is not such a change: that issue is only read, never edited.
  3. 3Otherwise preflight starts: the skill finds where the ticket will go. No question is asked before the first preview, with two exceptions (arrows 5 and 9).

Read the notes

  1. 4Preflight finds the Jira site through the Atlassian MCP (the connector that lets Claude read and write Jira) and the project key: from the notes first, then from issue keys like ABC-123 in the branch name, recent commits or the project's instruction files. A key found in the repository is used only when Jira confirms a project with exactly that key; otherwise the project becomes a numbered question. When Jira is not connected, the skill still drafts, and its first question asks you to connect it.
  2. 5The notes give no hint of what is wrong or needed, so no Problem can be written (for an epic, no Outcome). The skill says so and asks for one or two sentences. It does not fill the gap itself.
  3. 6Your answer joins the notes, and the reading starts again.
  4. 7The skill picks the type: a Story when someone gets a result, a Bug when something that exists works wrongly, a Task for work no user sees. It drafts an Epic only on your word. It also looks for separate items: things with a result of their own that a person can check and use without the others, such as two failures, or a failure and a new result. An "and" inside one result, its steps or its technical layers are never a reason to split, and when the skill is not sure, the items are not separate. Then it lists what the ticket needs and the notes do not give, sorted by who knows it: the code, Jira, or only you. When one reading of the notes is clearly more likely, it takes that reading and names it under Flags.
  5. 8You asked for a batch: several drafts in one preview, created by one yes. There are three kinds. Stories under an epic: "an epic, and let's add stories to it", or "add stories to ABC-12". Several standalone tickets: "create several tickets", or a list of tickets; this is a split, drafted at once with no Split: line (the line under a draft that offers to make separate tickets). Sub-tasks you list by name: "story X with sub-tasks A, B", or "add sub-tasks to ABC-7". "Create an epic" alone makes the epic and nothing more.
  6. 9The batch goes under an existing epic or parent such as ABC-12, and your notes name no items. The skill reads ABC-12 first, then asks for the list, one line each, before any preview. It never invents stories or sub-tasks.
  7. 10Each item takes its type by the same rules as a single ticket (arrow 7), or from your word. A child sits exactly one level below its parent: an epic holds stories, tasks and bugs, and a story, task or bug holds sub-tasks. "Story X with tasks A, B" therefore means sub-tasks. When you ask for a structure that breaks this rule, the skill drafts the closest one that fits (a Task under a Story becomes a sub-task, a sub-task under an epic becomes a Task) and says so under Flags. You asked for several tickets but the notes hold one result: it drafts one ticket and asks for your list of tickets, or 'one ticket'. It never cuts one result into parts itself. With a new epic or parent and no items named, it drafts the parent and asks for the list as a numbered question in the preview.
  8. 11Otherwise the run makes one ticket, an epic included when you asked for one.

Gather and draft

  1. 12The skill reads Jira itself: the work types the project offers with their levels, every field of each drafted type with its default and allowed values, the person the notes name, and you as the current user. One code scout (an agent with a fresh context) runs only when three things hold: the session is inside the repository the ticket is about, a fact is missing that the code holds, and that fact changes what the ticket says. It is read-only, stops after about ten tool calls, and returns facts with file:line, for example "the account page lists loans without due dates — web/account/loans.tsx:27". There is never a second scout. When it fails, the ticket is written from the notes alone.
  2. 13The project has no epic type, so it cannot hold an epic or a story batch, or it has no sub-task type and you asked for sub-tasks. The skill says so in one line and stops. For any other type the project lacks, it drafts with the closest type it offers and asks about it.
  3. 14The draft is in English whatever language the notes use; a label or an error text the product shows stays as shown, in quotes. Then one check, with no agent and no loop: one role and one goal (one "As a … I want …"), and a second clearly separate item stays out of the draft (on the Split: line, as its own batch item, or under Flags); a "so that" reason from the notes, or none and a flag; criteria a person can check as yes or no, with a number only when the notes give one; at most five criteria, or a flag "may be too big for one story". An epic is checked for one outcome and one to three "Done when" signals instead. In a batch, every title must differ from its siblings by its result.
  4. 15In the same turn, when Jira is connected and the project is known, an agent starts in the background to look for duplicate or related issues. It runs at most two Jira searches and never writes. The preview does not wait for it. When the draft has a Split: line, or the run is a split, the one search covers every item, and each candidate names the item it matches. A later 'split' needs no second search.
  5. 16The preview shows the draft between two horizontal lines and nothing else there. It opens with Type:, then Summary:, then the body. A batch shows the parent (a new one in full, an existing one as one line), then the list of titles such as S1. [Story] Member sees which loans are due soon, then each item's body. Under the second line come the Split: line when the notes hold separate items, the numbered Fields, each with where its value came from, the numbered Questions, Flags, Empty sections (a new epic only), the Context line (what was read), and the closing line. The labels follow your language; the ticket stays in English.
  6. 17The skill stops and waits for you.
  7. 18When the search finds candidates, a short message lists them: "1. S1 — LIB-310 — Show due dates on the loans list — To Do — related — same account page, due dates only. Say which to link (for example 'link 1'), or ignore this." For a single ticket, a 'link 1' works like a change: the next preview shows it as a Links line, so your yes covers it. In a batch, links wait until the issues exist. When nothing is found, nothing is said.

Your reply

  1. 19A clear yes ("yes", "create", "ok", "go") with no question open creates what the preview showed. A field line you did not change counts as accepted. A yes to a preview with a Split: line creates only the draft (S1): the other items on the line are not created. Silence or a new topic is never a yes.
  2. 20You answer the Split: line: 'split', 'epic' when the line offers it, or 'one ticket'.
  3. 21'split' brings a split: the draft as S1, then each other item in its full shape. 'epic' brings a story batch: a new epic drafted from the notes, with the items as its children; a Bug stays a Bug. 'one ticket' drops the line and names each other item under Flags as Bug "Renew button is grey on mobile": not in this ticket and not created; reply 'split' to add it. Jira is read first for any type not read yet, and nothing is created in the same turn.
  4. 22An answer or a change: by number ("10 Central", "3 High"), by item id ("S2 drop", "S3 Task", "S4" with a new title), new text, another type, site or project.
  5. 23The skill applies it, reads Jira again when a type, the site, the project or a batch's parent changed, and shows the full preview again. It never creates in the same turn. Item ids are numbered again from S1. Dropping every story or sub-task leaves the new parent as a single ticket; under an existing parent it leaves nothing to create, so the skill says so and stops. Dropping all tickets of a split but one leaves a single ticket.
  6. 24A yes while a question is still open brings the full preview again, with the question still open. Nothing is created.
  7. 25A reply the skill cannot read as a yes or a change: it asks what to change, and your answer comes back to the same choice.

Create

  1. 26The skill creates the issue through the Atlassian MCP with exactly what the preview showed, and sends only fields that have a value. A batch creates the new epic or parent first, when it has one, then each item in S order with that key as its parent; in a split, each ticket takes the parent the notes name, or none. An existing epic or parent is never edited.
  2. 27Jira returned a key for every call. One line: Created LIB-482: <link>. After a yes to a preview with a Split: line it adds · not created: S2 [Bug] Renew button is grey on mobile. A story batch: Created PAY-40: <link> · stories: PAY-41, PAY-42, PAY-43. A split: Created S1 LIB-510: <link> · S2 LIB-511: <link>.
  3. 28A call failed. Either Jira rejected it, and the skill shows Jira's exact error, changes nothing itself and asks for the value Jira wants; or the call timed out or returned no key, and the issue may exist, so the skill does not retry and gives the summary to search for. A batch stops at the first failed call and reports what was created and what was not. A single ticket with no key ends here.
  4. 29Your reply brings a new preview: after a rejection, the value you give; in a batch, the items not yet created (one item left of a split shows as a single ticket). An item that returned no key stays out until you say "not there". When the new epic or parent itself returned no key, the skill first asks for its key, or "not there". A reply that drops the rest ends the run. Only a new yes creates anything.
  5. 30After the issue exists, you can link a found issue: "link 1", or after a batch "link 1 to LIB-511". The skill first checks that the site offers the link type, then adds Duplicate or Relates. It never links on silence.

Which type it picks

The type decides the shape of the body. The draft opens with a Type: line above the Summary, and line 2 of Fields repeats the type with its reason, so one reply changes both: 2 Bug.

The notes describeTypeThe body
A result someone will get, an end user or a colleagueStory (the usual case)User story ("As a … I want … so that …"), Problem, two or three acceptance criteria
Something that exists and works wronglyBugProblem, Steps to reproduce (only when the notes or the code scout give them), Expected result, Actual result
Work no user sees: an upgrade, a clean-up, a question to answer in a time limitTaskProblem, one to three acceptance criteria
One large outcome over several sprintsEpic, only when you askProblem, Outcome, Done when; Scope, Out of scope and Stories only when the notes give them
  • An epic only on your word. When the notes look like one big result, the skill drafts the closest other type. When the project has an epic type, line 2 of Fields then ends with "this looks like an epic — reply '2 Epic' to draft it as one".
  • Two types fit equally. It drafts the one higher in the table (a Story before a Bug) and asks about the other.
  • The project lacks the type. It drafts with the closest type the project offers and asks. With no epic type there is no epic and no story batch, and with no sub-task type no sub-tasks: the skill says so and stops.
  • A child sits one level below its parent. An epic holds stories, tasks and bugs; a story, task or bug holds sub-tasks. A Task under a Story becomes a sub-task, and a sub-task under an epic becomes a Task, with a line under Flags.

Items of a story batch or a sub-task batch are shorter, because the parent already gives the reason:

ItemIts body
A story in a batchThe user story and two criteria
A task in a batchOne or two sentences of Problem, one or two criteria
A sub-taskOne or two sentences: what is done and what it is for; no criteria, no headings
A bug in any batchThe full Bug shape: Problem, Steps to reproduce, Expected result, Actual result
A ticket in a splitIts full shape, because no epic gives the reason

Every Summary is a short statement with a verb, in the notes' own words, so a search finds it: "Member sees which loans are due soon", never "Loan due dates".

When it offers a split

An item is separate when it has a result of its own that a person can check and use without the other items. When the notes clearly hold two or more, the single-ticket preview drafts the one you asked for (or the first) and names the others on a Split: line, each with its type and a ready title.

The notes holdThe split it offers
Two or more failures of something that existsOne Bug each
A failure of something that exists, and a new resultA Bug and a Story (or a Task)
Results for different roles, or with different reasonsOne Story each
Work no user sees that the other items do not needA Task next to them

These never start a split, so the notes stay one ticket: an "and", "or" or "also" inside one result; the steps, paths, rules or data variants of one result; technical layers, and technical work the result needs; a part that gives nothing of value alone; a result that is only big (that gets the epic hint); a defect in work not done yet. When the skill is not sure, the items are not separate.

The three batch kinds

A batch is several drafts in one preview, created by one yes. It starts only from your request or from your 'split' or 'epic' reply. The items carry the ids S1, S2, … so you can change one with S2 drop or S3 Task.

Split"create several tickets", a list of tickets, or 'split' to the split offerThe tickets, each in its full shape; no parent of their ownEach ticket in S order

The titles come first, as one list (S1. [Story] Member pays a fine by card on the account page), so you read what each item delivers before any body. A batch stops at the first call Jira rejects or that returns no key, and reports what was created and what was not.

What makes it different

Every value has a source

A sentence with no source in the notes, the code or Jira is removed; a missing fact is named under Flags instead of guessed.

The result, never the route

The skill assumes that the developers who take the ticket break the work down and size it, so it writes no plan and proposes no sub-tasks. It drafts sub-tasks only when you list them. A fix your notes propose is kept under Proposed solution, marked as your idea.

A split is offered, never made on its own

An "and" in the notes is a weak sign of two items, so the skill drafts one ticket and puts the split on one line. A wrong guess costs one ignored line, and 'split' costs one word.

One result stays one ticket

The skill assumes that the team slices one result in refinement, close to the sprint that takes it, so it never cuts a result by its steps or layers.

An epic only on your word

Where a big story ends and an epic begins is your call, so the skill suggests an epic and lets you decide.

The type opens the draft

The type decides the shape of the body, and its numbered line in Fields lets one reply change it.

A child one level below its parent

Jira's default levels are epic, then story, task or bug, then sub-task, so a structure that skips a level is redrawn to the closest one that fits.

More than five criteria is a flag

A story that needs more than five criteria is likely more than one story, so the skill keeps the five most important and flags it.

A Summary with a verb

The Summary is what a board card shows and what a search matches, so it says what the ticket delivers, in the words your notes use.

One preview, numbered lines

Every value is visible and every number points at one line, so one reply can answer or change any of them.

A yes creates only what you saw

Silence approves the values, never the create, and any change shows the whole preview again.

One pass, not research

At most one agent reads the code, and the check runs once. A deeper question belongs before the ticket.

Duplicates in the background

The preview never waits for the search, one search covers every item, and a link is made only when you name it.

No retry without a key

The issue may already exist, so a retry could create it twice.
